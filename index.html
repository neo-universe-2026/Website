        The jukebox reads each frame of a video and turns what it sees into music. Sound is still being built; this is the plan.

        Colour becomes harmony
        Warm colours lean towards bright, major chords. Cool colours lean towards darker, minor ones.
        Brightness becomes pitch
        Bright areas play higher notes. Dark areas play lower notes.
        Busy areas become rhythm
        Crowded, detailed parts of the picture play faster rhythms. Calm, empty parts slow down.
        Change over time becomes a story
        As the video moves on, the music changes with it, so you hear the planet change.
        Separate layers become separate instruments
        When a video shows several things at once, such as smoke, dust and sea salt, each can get its own instrument.

        Each dataset on the globe has its own "How it will sound" section with the plan for that video.

        Three questions about how the data will sound.

        The challenge

        NASA's Earth Information Center makes striking visualizations of our changing planet: temperatures, ocean currents, forests, storms. Pictures alone leave out blind and low-vision people. The Earth Information Jukebox challenge asks teams to turn these visuals into sound, so everyone can explore the data.

        Our approach

        We start from NASA's own videos. Each one becomes a soundtrack, where colour, brightness and movement in the picture control harmony, pitch and rhythm. This site puts every dataset on a globe you can spin, and every page works with a screen reader and the keyboard.
